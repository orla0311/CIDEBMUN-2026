Economic and Social Council

BeginnerRegularEnglish

ECOSOC coordinates the UN's economic, social and development work and reviews progress on the Sustainable Development Goals. It writes no binding law, but it sets the analytical frame — the numbers, definitions and recommendations that finance ministries and lenders later argue over.

Topic A

Addressing Global Debt Distress in Developing Economies

More than half of low-income countries are in debt distress or at high risk of it, and dozens spend more servicing external debt than on health or education. Restructuring is harder than in the 1990s because creditors are fragmented between bilateral state lenders, private bondholders and multilateral banks — and every case stalls on who takes the first loss.

Questions a resolution must answer
  • How should private bondholders be brought into a restructuring they can currently sit out?
  • Should relief be conditional, and on what — fiscal targets, transparency, social spending floors?
  • What role should debt-for-climate or debt-for-nature swaps play, and at what scale are they real?
  • Who arbitrates comparability of treatment between bilateral and multilateral creditors?
Topic B

Promoting Foreign Direct Investment and Financial Inclusion in Developing Economies

Capital does not flow where it is scarcest: investment concentrates in a few middle-income markets while the countries with the youngest populations and largest infrastructure gaps are priced as if failure were certain. Financial inclusion is the same problem at household scale — mobile money has brought millions their first account, but an account is not affordable credit.

Questions a resolution must answer
  • What guarantees or blended-finance instruments actually lower the cost of capital, and who funds them?
  • How can investment incentives be offered without triggering a tax and labour race to the bottom?
  • What consumer protections should accompany mobile lending and digital credit scoring?
  • How should local content, technology transfer and profit repatriation be balanced?
Welcome letter

A letter from the dais

Dear delegates,

Welcome to the Economic and Social Council. Your two topics — debt distress and the flow of investment into developing economies — are questions of arithmetic before they are questions of principle. A government servicing debt at the cost of its health budget is making a choice, and so is every creditor that declines to take the first loss.

Come prepared with your country's actual position: what it owes, what it lends, what it needs. We expect resolutions with mechanisms in them — who pays, who verifies, on what timetable — rather than statements of concern. Bring numbers you can defend.

The dais — Economic and Social Council

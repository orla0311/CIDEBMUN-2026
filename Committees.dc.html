Section 01

Regular committees

Standard parliamentary procedure: speakers list, moderated and unmoderated caucus, working papers, amendments, vote. Twenty delegates per room. Each committee has its own page.

Human Rights Council

BeginnerRegularSpanish

The Council is the UN's principal human rights body: forty-seven elected members who investigate violations, hold universal periodic reviews and appoint special rapporteurs. It cannot compel a state to act, so its power is documentary and political — the record it builds, and the cost of appearing in that record. This committee debates in Spanish.

Economic and Social Council

BeginnerRegularEnglish

ECOSOC coordinates the UN's economic, social and development work and reviews progress on the Sustainable Development Goals. It writes no binding law, but it sets the analytical frame — the numbers, definitions and recommendations that finance ministries and lenders later argue over.

World Health Organization

BeginnerRegularEnglish

The WHO sets health norms, declares public health emergencies of international concern and coordinates response — with a budget largely made of earmarked voluntary contributions, which means its priorities are shaped by whoever funds them.

General Assembly

IntermediateRegularEnglish

The only organ where every member state has one vote and no one holds a veto. Its resolutions are recommendations, which makes the size of the majority the whole point: a text carried by consensus reads very differently from one carried by fifty-one percent.

Commission on the Status of Women

AdvancedRegularEnglish

A functional commission of ECOSOC, and the body that produced the Beijing Platform for Action. It negotiates agreed conclusions by consensus, which makes language itself the battleground — a single bracketed phrase can hold a text for days.

United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime

IntermediateRegularEnglish

The UN's lead office on illicit drugs, organised crime and corruption. Delegates take on transnational trafficking and the financial networks that launder its profits.

Section 02

Special, crisis & joint committees

Rooms with their own rules. Delegates hold named roles rather than country placards, directives replace or accompany resolutions, and the crisis staff can change the situation between sessions. Each committee has its own page.

Security Council

IntermediateCrisis · RegularSpanish

Quince miembros, cinco con veto, y el único órgano cuyas decisiones son jurídicamente obligatorias. Este comité corre en español y puede recibir actualizaciones de crisis en cualquier momento de la sesión.

International Criminal Court

AdvancedCrisisEnglish

A permanent court of last resort under the Rome Statute, prosecuting individuals — not states — for genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and aggression. It acts only where national courts are unwilling or unable to, and it has no police force of its own.

Joint War Committee

IntermediateJoint

Two linked chambers whose decisions affect each other. Delegates sit as military and political representatives of the Allied Powers, and a directive passed in one room becomes the situation the other room inherits.

The Ministry of Magic

BeginnerSpecialEnglish

A fictional-setting committee run on real procedure. Delegates hold ministerial and departmental posts rather than country placards, and debate the governance of a magical community under threat — the same institutional questions a beginner would meet in a UN room, in a setting that makes them easier to argue about.

United States Senate

AdvancedSpecialEnglish

A legislative committee, not a diplomatic one. Delegates sit as senators bound to a party and a state, and the output is a bill that must survive committee markup, amendment on the floor, cloture at sixty votes and a conference with the House.
